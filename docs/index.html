ALPHAURBAN · INSIDE THE FIELD SINGAPORE · 2024

ONE FIELD · 36,864 CELLS · 64 NUMBERS EACH

What does a
planetary embedding
keep of one city?

AlphaEarth compresses every place on Earth into 64 numbers, and it separates one city from another easily. This walk follows one 36,864-cell field — Singapore, Johor Bahru and Batam — into that representation to see what the same 64 numbers hold of the differences inside a single city.

One field in one year, described cell by cell, then set among 1,000 cities measured the same way.

01 / 10

The field in the embedding's own colours.

No land-cover legend was used to draw this map. Each cell takes its colour from three of AlphaEarth's own 64 dimensions, and a geography no one labelled comes back on its own — coastlines, forest, the built strip along the strait. The rest of the walk asks how much structure the same 64 dimensions show inside that built strip.

Components A39, A62 and A08 mapped to red, green and blue — the canonical city view — display-stretched over land.

02 / 10

Seven land-cover classes across three countries.

The same field under a human legend: Singapore in the centre, Johor Bahru across the strait to the north, Batam to the south. ESA WorldCover names seven kinds of land here, and the same built and forest classes appear on all three sides of the strait.

03 / 10

The land account.

Set the permanent water aside — 14,962 cells of strait and sea — and pack what remains by class. The land becomes an account: forest and built land dominate, and over a third of the land cells are built. Those built cells are what the rest of the walk follows.

04 / 10

Every cell becomes 64 numbers.

AlphaEarth replaces each cell with a unit vector — a direction on a 64-dimensional sphere. Shown here through one fixed 3-D projection, the broad land-cover classes separate on their own.

Positions: fixed PCA display projection, fit on land cells. Every quoted number is computed in the native 64 dimensions.

05 / 10

Greenness organises the field.

Recolour the same points by measured vegetation (NDVI) and the cloud reads as one smooth gradient: the embedding's dominant axis tracks how green the land is. Vegetation is a reasonable thing for a planetary representation to encode strongly, but it does not separate one part of a city from another.

06 / 10

The greenness account.

Pack the land cells again, this time ordered by measured NDVI rather than by class. The field becomes one continuous gradient from bare and built to dense forest, with no natural break where "city" ends. Where the legend puts a boundary, the measured gradient runs straight through.

07 / 10

Now look inside the built class.

Built cells differ widely among themselves. Recoloured by built volume, they run from low sheds and terraces to the dense cores of the CBD and the HDB towns — differences a city plans around, and ones a vegetation index cannot express.

08 / 10

The built-volume account.

Order just the 8,293 built cells by their built volume, from low sheds and terraces up to the dense cores. A single class in the land legend contains the full range of urban density, and that within-class variation is what the audit asks the embedding to carry.

Colour is square-root-scaled to the built 98th percentile, so the gradient shows order, not the shape of the distribution.

09 / 10

How much of that does the field keep?

In the native 64 dimensions, the entire built class occupies a pocket about as wide as any single natural class, and the mean directions of low-rise and high-rise Singapore sit closer together than forest sits to water. Forms that differ sharply on the ground occupy neighbouring directions here.

Descriptive geometry for one field and one year, and no verdict is drawn from it.

10 / 10

Back to the map.

From above, the built area reads as one continuous fabric. Inside the representation, the differences separating one part of that fabric from another are the finest in the field, and no global label set records them at this scale. The audit therefore reads the representation itself.

11 / 18

The same walk in Mexico City.

Mexico City is a monocentric metropolis in a high basin, and differs in form from the island city-state in almost every respect. It is walked with the same satellite, the same embedding and the same steps, at double speed.

12 / 18

Both cities in a shared projection.

Both fields lift into embedding space through a single shared projection, fit on the land cells of both cities together, so what you compare is the representation and not two camera angles. The broad classes organise both clouds the same way.

13 / 18

Leave the cities' own frame.

Every view so far used a projection built to spread one or two cities out. Switch to the frame the planet defines — axes fit on the mean directions of 1,000 cities — and the internal detail of each city gathers into a compact region of the sphere.

Display change only: the cells' 64-D coordinates are identical in both frames.

14 / 18

Each city reduced to one direction.

Take the mean direction of every cell in a city: one point for Singapore, one for Mexico City, 85.6° apart on the embedding sphere. The planetary audit compares cities as these single directions.

15 / 18

One thousand cities at once.

Every city now has its point: 1,000 functional urban areas, each reduced to its 2024 mean direction. Coloured by continent, the globe organises immediately, with cities from the same continent falling near one another.

16 / 18

Köppen climate separates the cities too.

Recolour the same points by Köppen climate family and the globe reorganises just as cleanly. Much of what separates cities in the embedding is environmental context: the same greenness-and-climate signal that organised the field inside Singapore.

17 / 18

Population separates cities less clearly.

Colour by population quintile and the pattern is weaker: megacities and small cities interleave. Environment and geography account for more of the arrangement than the human scale of the settlement does.

18 / 18

Cities occupy their own corner of the world.

The grey density behind the points is the rest of the land: 273,410 non-city pixels sampled across the planet. Cities cluster away from it, in a region of their own, and sit a median 73.7° apart from one another — while Singapore's own cells spread 37.4° around its mean and Mexico City's 33.9°. The embedding separates cities from the world and from each other by wide margins. What those margins cost the detail inside each city is the open question.

Descriptive numbers: FUA city support, 2024, equal weight per city. Grey density: non-city terrestrial pixels (GHS-SMOD below 21), same projection and camera as the points.

FROM TWO FIELDS TO ONE THOUSAND

Cities will be read through this field.

Planetary embeddings are built once and used everywhere — for land, water, crops, change, and for cities, whether or not the field holds their detail. In both fields the embedding held cities far apart while each city's interior gathered close, and the same geometry organised all 1,000. What the margin between cities costs the neighbourhoods within them is the question that remains.
