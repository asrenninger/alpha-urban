ONE FIELD · 36,864 CELLS · 64 NUMBERS EACH

What does a
planetary embedding
keep of one city?

AlphaEarth compresses every place on Earth into 64 numbers, and it separates one city from another easily. This walk follows one 36,864-cell field — Singapore, Johor Bahru and Batam — into that representation to see what the same 64 numbers hold of the differences inside a single city.

One field in one year, described cell by cell, then set among 1,000 cities measured the same way.

The land, packed by class.

Set the permanent water aside — 14,962 cells of strait and sea — and pack what remains by class. Forest and built land dominate, and over a third of the land cells are built. Those built cells are what the rest of the walk follows.

04 / 19

Every cell becomes 64 numbers.

AlphaEarth replaces each cell with a unit vector — a direction on a 64-dimensional sphere. Shown here through one fixed 3-D projection, the broad land-cover classes separate on their own.

Positions come from one fixed projection, fit on land cells. Every number is computed in all 64 dimensions.

05 / 19

Greenness organises the field.

Recolour the same points by measured vegetation (NDVI) and the cloud reads as one smooth gradient: the embedding's dominant axis tracks how green the land is. Vegetation is a reasonable thing for a planetary representation to encode strongly, but it does not separate one part of a city from another.

06 / 19

The land, ordered by greenness.

Pack the land cells again, this time ordered by measured NDVI rather than by class. The field becomes one continuous gradient from bare and built to dense forest, with no natural break where "city" ends.

07 / 19

Now look inside the built class.

Built cells differ widely among themselves. Recoloured by built volume, they run from low sheds and terraces to the dense cores of the city centre and the housing towns — differences a city plans around, and ones a vegetation index cannot express.

08 / 19

Built cells, ordered by volume.

Order just the 8,293 built cells by their built volume, from low sheds and terraces up to the dense cores. A single class in the land legend contains the full range of urban density, and that variation is what we ask the embedding to carry.

Colour is square-root-scaled to the built 98th percentile, so the gradient shows order, not the shape of the distribution.

09 / 19

How much of that does the field keep?

In all 64 dimensions, the entire built class occupies a pocket about as wide as any single natural class, and the mean directions of low-rise and high-rise Singapore sit closer together than forest sits to water.

These numbers describe one field in one year.

10 / 19

Back to the map.

From above, the built area reads as one continuous fabric. Inside the representation, the differences separating one part of that fabric from another are the finest in the field, and no global label set records them at this scale. So what follows measures the representation directly.

14 / 19

Each cloud, summarised as vectors.

A direction on this sphere is a vector from its centre, and the arrows summarise each cloud: the bold arrow points to the mean of the city's urban cells, the thin arrows to the mean of each degree of urbanisation. At this scale the thin arrows hug the bold one: every degree of urbanisation in a city points almost the same way. The next scene keeps only the tips.

In the global frame, 2024, each degree sits 0.5–6° from the mean of its city's urban cells; in the two-city frame the same degrees spread about 25°. Degrees with too few valid pixels are omitted, and Mexico City's mean lies on the far side of the sphere, so its arrows are drawn fainter.

15 / 19

Each city reduced to one direction.

Take the mean direction of every cell in a city: one point for Singapore, one for Mexico City, 85.6° apart on the embedding sphere. From here on, each city is one such direction.

16 / 19

One thousand cities at once.

Every city now has its point: 1,000 cities, each reduced to its mean direction in 2024. Coloured by continent, the globe organises immediately, with cities from the same continent falling near one another.

17 / 19

Climate separates the cities too.

Recolour the same points by climate and the globe reorganises just as cleanly. Much of what separates cities in the embedding is environment: the same greenness and climate that organised the field inside Singapore.

18 / 19

Population separates cities less clearly.

Colour by population quintile and the pattern is weaker: megacities and small cities interleave. Environment and geography arrange the cities more than population does.

About this experiment

A country-weighted 64 → 128 → 64 residual adapter nudges existing AlphaEarth embeddings toward three weighted goals. One architecture and a fixed 24-epoch horizon were selected by three-fold country-held-out validation, then held constant across the surface. It does not retrain AlphaEarth or impose a geometry-preservation penalty.

The regular 66-model lattice and exact equal-priority fit define the base surface. Two explicitly post-audit, 5%-spaced knots refine the nonlinear edge near pure building volume. Between anchors, the browser blends neighbouring three-seed mean representations before the fixed projection. Twelve separately trained off-grid fits audit the refined interpolation: all 12 were within 5% of their trained objective, with mean cosine similarity 0.995. Scores between the 79 evaluated settings are labelled estimates.

The sphere is a visual-only common-support layer: 15,970 deterministic rows retain all 998 eligible cities, 162 countries and 998 city-years from 501,969 rows with all three observed, non-water outcomes. It is not the analytical sample. Score cards use every target-valid held-out row: 216,504 NDVI pixels, 138,473 volume pixels and 1,036,656 non-water land-cover pixels. Land cover begins with 3,982,891 rows; 224,503 permanent-water rows are excluded, leaving 3,758,388. Colours show observations and never model predictions.

The sphere is one fixed three-dimensional projection of the 64-dimensional ensemble means. Visible separation is not the prediction score. The original test countries were excluded from every v3 fit and selection step, but they had already been inspected during v2, so these results are confirmatory rather than a new sealed evaluation. Any best point is descriptive, not a test-selected recommendation.

About these city fields

Each field is a rectangle of 192 × 192 cells around a city. Their widths differ, so the maps have different scales. All cells with valid embeddings are shown, including water. Missing measurements appear grey.

Each pair shares one projection, fit on the land cells of both fields, with the same axes and camera on both sides. The spheres show a projection of the 64-dimensional representation; distances on screen are not distances in the full embedding.

Embeddings and Sentinel-2 NDVI: 2024. ESA WorldCover: 2021. GHSL Degree of Urbanisation: 2020. Building volume: DLR WSF3D. NDVI and volume use a common colour scale across all eight fields; volume is square-root scaled and capped at the 98th percentile of all built cells.

The similarity readouts are calculated for every valid embedding in the two displayed fields, including water. Mean direction distance is the angle between the two mean directions in 64 dimensions. Variation overlap compares the ten main directions of spread in each field. It measures alignment of variation, not equality of the whole distributions; two random sets of ten directions overlap by about 15.9%.

Each curve has area one, and both cities use the same bins and smoothing. Building volume is shown on a log axis, using uncapped measurements. Missing measurements are excluded from that variable only, with coverage shown beside each city. Urbanisation and land cover show exact shares. Embedding spread is each pixel’s angular distance from its own field’s mean direction. These comparisons describe pixels in the displayed fields, whose extents differ; they do not compare whole-city area or population.
